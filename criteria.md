# Elpys listing criteria

Read this in full before sorting any candidate. The criteria are short; the practical
disqualifiers underneath them are where most orgs actually fail.

## 1. The six criteria

**1. No adult supervision required on-site.** A teen must be able to attend
independently. A one-time parent-signed waiver completed at home is acceptable. A parent
who has to physically attend is not. This is the criterion most candidates fail, and it
is often stated only in a buried FAQ line rather than on the signup page.

**2. Clear, completable sign-up process.** The steps must be knowable and doable by a
teen without a phone call to figure out what's going on. "Fill out our interest form and
someone will be in touch" is acceptable only if the org actually responds and the path is
described. Vague is a reject.

**3. Online signup preferred; email or phone signup acceptable.** Email/phone signups are
real listings but go in the **"Contact to sign up"** section (`section = contact`) rather
than the online section (`section = online`). Don't reject an org for lacking a portal.

**4. Within roughly 20 minutes of the anchor location.** Measured as drive time, not
straight-line distance. Where a teen can't drive, note transit access.

**5. Open to teens.** No strict age floor is set, but middle and high school age is the
target. 18+ only is a reject. Narrow windows (16–17) are listable if stated clearly.
When no age is published at all, apply the unstated-age rule in `SKILL.md` step 5 rather
than guessing — "all community members welcome" and "apply to be considered" point to
different verdicts.

**6. Free.** Programs with fees are excluded, or listed only with the fee flagged
explicitly. Kelsey Creek's Summer Teen Farm Program ($60 resident / $72 non-resident) is
treated as a paid camp, not volunteering.

## 2. Disqualifiers observed in practice

Each of these has killed at least one real candidate:

- Long application plus interview process
- Multi-month minimum commitment (judgment call — some are listed with a clear warning)
- Background checks with long waits (Hopelink is listed but flagged)
- Any fee
- Sold-out programs, or application windows currently closed
- Being a directory rather than an actual opportunity (mine it, don't list it)
- Mandatory multi-hour orientation before a first shift
- Adult required on-site
- Age floor above the high school range
- No volunteer information published anywhere findable

## 5. Accuracy corrections — do not regress these

Every one of these was found by checking a live page against an assumption:

- **Food Lifeline:** solo age is **16+**, not 14+. Ages 10–15 need an adult present the
  entire time.
- **WTA:** the annual waiver is **printed and brought to the trailhead**, signed by both
  youth and guardian. It is not completed online.
- **King County Parks:** the youth waiver is **downloaded, printed, and brought to the
  event**, and is not truly one-time.
- **KidVantage:** minimum age is 12 with an adult; 14+ may volunteer solo.
- **Kelsey Creek Farm:** two distinct programs — After School Teen Volunteers (12–18,
  adult needed for 12–13) and Saturday Morning Teen Volunteers (15–18). The Summer Teen
  Farm Program carries a fee. Farm Fair event volunteering is free.
- **Hopelink:** a background check is required regardless of age and takes up to a week,
  before orientation. Sequence: online application → background check → orientation
  invite → shifts. Three-month minimum commitment.
- **tBUG address:** `1608 156th Pl NE` — not "156th Ave SE," which is a different street
  on the opposite side of Bellevue.
- **City of Sammamish:** welcomes ages 8+; volunteers 13 and under need an adult directly
  supervising. Events need a minimum signup count to run.
- **Sophia Way signup link:** `https://volunteer.bloomerang.co/JE/uxxmhf89pf6yta` is
  correct. A search snippet once suggested a VolunteerLocal URL; it was stale. **Always
  verify against the live page, never trust a snippet over a direct fetch.**

The pattern across all of these: the org's marketing page says one thing and the FAQ,
waiver, or portal says another. The stricter, more specific source is usually right.

## 6. Verification checklist per candidate

Answer each with a fact and a source URL, or mark it unknown:

- [ ] Can a teen attend without a parent physically present?
- [ ] What is the minimum age solo? Accompanied?
- [ ] Is there a fee of any kind?
- [ ] What are the exact signup steps, in order?
- [ ] Does the signup link resolve to a working form?
- [ ] Is there an application, interview, background check, or orientation?
- [ ] Is there a minimum commitment?
- [ ] Is the program currently running, or seasonal/paused/full?
- [ ] What is the address, or is it rotating locations?
- [ ] Is it within ~20 minutes of the anchor? Transit-reachable?
- [ ] Contact email and phone?
- [ ] Does any waiver need to be printed and brought along?
