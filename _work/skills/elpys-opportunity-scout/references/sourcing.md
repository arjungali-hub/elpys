# Where candidates actually come from

Ordered roughly by yield. The high-yield sources are municipal and civic; national
volunteer platforms look promising but mostly surface 18+ roles and duplicate listings.

## Tier 1 — reliably produces listable opportunities

**City parks and recreation departments.** Every city in the region runs volunteer
events — restoration work parties, park cleanups, farm and garden programs, event
support. These are episodic, free, and frequently teen-friendly. Search
`site:<city>.gov volunteer` and `<city> parks volunteer` and check the recreation
department's events calendar directly, not just the volunteer landing page.

**City-run farms, gardens, and nature centers.** Kelsey Creek, Bellevue Botanical Garden,
and tBUG all came from this category. Look for demonstration gardens, P-Patches, community
orchards, nature centers, and heritage farms.

**Environmental restoration nonprofits.** EarthCorps, WTA, Forterra, Mountains to Sound
Greenway, EarthGen, local watershed and stream-keeper groups, Green Kirkland / Green
Redmond / Green Sammamish partnerships. Work parties are the single best shape of
opportunity for teens: one-off, free, outdoors, signup-and-show-up.

**Food banks and food access programs.** Nearly every city has one, and sorting shifts
are usually open to teens. Check the age policy carefully — it varies more than anything
else in this category and is frequently misstated on aggregator sites.

**Farmers markets.** Seasonal, weekly, and consistently teen-friendly. Search
`<city> farmers market volunteer`.

**Libraries.** King County Library System branches run teen advisory boards, summer
reading helpers, and book sales. Check the specific branch, not just the KCLS system page.

## Tier 2 — worth checking, mixed results

**Thrift stores run by nonprofits.** Jubilee REACH came from here. Sorting and stocking
roles, often flexible on age. Check for background-check requirements.

**Animal shelters and rescues.** Frequently blocked by age floors (often 16 or 18) or
fees, but worth a check per city.

**Senior centers and meal delivery programs.** Often welcoming, sometimes require a
background check or a driving volunteer.

**Museums and historical societies.** Event-day volunteering is often open; ongoing
docent roles usually aren't.

**Hospital and clinic junior volunteer programs.** Almost always blocked by long
applications, orientations, or hour minimums — but the exceptions are strong listings.

**Community and cultural festivals.** Highly seasonal. Worth listing when the signup
window is open; note the window in the listing.

## Tier 3 — mine, don't list

These are directories. Extract organization names from them, then verify those orgs
directly. Never list the directory itself.

- United Way of King County volunteer portal
- JustServe
- VolunteerMatch, Idealist, Golden
- The original KCLS teen volunteering list
- City "community resources" pages that just link out

## Search patterns that work

Vary these with the anchor city's name and its neighboring towns:

- `<city> teen volunteer opportunities`
- `<city> youth volunteer age 14`
- `site:<city>.gov volunteer`
- `<city> parks work party`
- `<city> food bank volunteer age requirement`
- `<city> farmers market volunteer`
- `"volunteer" "<city>" "16 and older" OR "14 and older"`
- `<county> parks youth volunteer waiver`

Two patterns that pay off disproportionately:

**Search the age, not the activity.** Queries phrased around a specific age ("volunteer
age 14 <city>") surface the FAQ pages where age policies actually live, which is exactly
the fact hardest to confirm later.

**Follow the portal.** When several orgs in a city use the same volunteer portal
(Bloomerang, VolunteerHub, Golden, SignUpGenius), browsing that portal's public event list
for the area often surfaces orgs that have no discoverable volunteer page of their own.

## Notes on scoping a new city

When expanding to a new city, the municipal government is the first stop and usually
yields two or three listings on its own. Do that before any general web searching — it
sets a baseline and reveals which nonprofits the city partners with, which is itself a
candidate list.
